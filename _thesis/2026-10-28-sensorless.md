This thesis concerns the computation of the high-frequency parameters of a motor with a
salient rotor designed for sensorless control, together with their experimental validation
on a prototype.

At low and zero speed, sensorless drives usually estimate the rotor position by injecting a
high-frequency signal and exploiting the magnetic saliency of the rotor. The behavior of
the machine at high frequency therefore determines how well the position can be detected.
In this work the parameters will be computed by means of numerical simulations, and the
results will be compared with measurements carried out on a prototype in the laboratory
test bench.

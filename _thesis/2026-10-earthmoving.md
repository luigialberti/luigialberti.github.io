This thesis focuses on control systems based on electric drives for earth-moving machinery,
such as excavators, with the aim of improving the efficiency, the precision and the safety
of these machines.

The electrification of construction machinery is opening new possibilities in the way
hydraulic actuators are supplied and controlled. Within this field, the thesis can address
different topics depending on the interests of the student. One option is the modeling of
the hydraulic system, as a tool to understand where energy is lost and how to reduce these
losses. Another is the design of controllers that regulate the hydraulic actuators by means
of electric drives, possibly within a distributed hydraulics architecture in which each
actuator has its own pump and motor. The work may also deal with the control of positioning
systems and with drive-by-wire solutions, where the commands of the operator reach the
actuators through an electronic control system.

In all cases, attention will be paid to the safety of the machine and to its overall energy
balance.
